# Research Depth Checks

Use these checks from framing through synthesis. Apply only those that could
change the answer, confidence, or next action. They guide research and do not
create required sections in the briefing.

## Foundations

- Separate verified facts from assumptions and constraints.
- Ask what mechanism supports each material claim and what conditions it needs.
- Trace the mechanism to the baseline, a lens return, or the source audit. Label
  unsupported steps as inference with High, Medium, or Low confidence.

## System context

- Choose a boundary and time horizon suited to the research purpose. Note a
  relevant larger system when it could change the answer.
- Distinguish an observed pattern over time from a single snapshot.
- Examine material actors, relationships, incentives, resources, and
  constraints. State how one element affects another rather than listing them.
- Trace direct and downstream effects link by link. A supported start and end
  do not prove an unsupported link between them.
- Examine feedback, delays, nonlinear effects, path dependence, accumulation,
  capacity limits, spillovers, displaced costs, or adaptation only when the
  record supports them. Do not invent a dynamic to fill a checklist.
- When evaluating an intervention, test relevant time horizons, trade-offs,
  distributional effects, monitoring signals, and conditions for revision.

Formal diagrams, stock-and-flow models, equations, and simulations are separate
deliverables produced only when requested.

## Presentation and degradation

Integrate material findings into the reader's natural sections. Omit depth
concepts that add nothing. When source access, lens coverage, or context
isolation is degraded, state how that limits the affected claim and confidence.
