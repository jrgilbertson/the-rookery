# Briefing Template

Read this file only when the user requests a full briefing or long-form work
such as an article, paper, blog post, presentation, or report.

## Voice

Write as a precise, thoughtful human would: favor concrete details and active
verbs, use natural varied rhythm, and ruthlessly eliminate every unnecessary
word, abstraction, hedge, cliché, or formulaic AI phrase.

## Long-form outline

Produce an outline before prose. Include:

- a working thesis and section sequence;
- the key claim and needed evidence for each section;
- the tension or objection each section resolves;
- the evidence or reliability limit that must remain in the final draft.

## Section writing style

Use a consistent pyramid style in multi-point sections that list takeaways:

1. **Overview** opens with natural prose (situation → recommendation → what to
   do → why → material limits). An executive who stops after this section should
   still know the answer, the main next steps, the main reasons, and the main
   gaps or risks. Put the actionable implication here—do not park it in a
   separate section by default. Everything after Overview **supports** that
   lead; it must not reveal the decision or next steps for the first time.
2. After that prose, list **key takeaways** as bullets. Include the decision and
   the concrete next steps among those takeaways when the intended use calls for
   action. For exploratory briefings with no action ask, state what is known and
   what remains open without forcing a to-do list.
3. In each bullet, the **first sentence is the takeaway and is bold**. The rest
   of the bullet is normal text that explains, supports, or bounds that
   takeaway.

Apply the same bold-takeaway-plus-support bullet pattern to **Key tensions**,
**Agreement and gaps**, and **Most reliable findings**.

**Perspective scan** is different on purpose. Give each lens its own short
subsection (not a one-line bullet). Include the question that lens asked, what
it found, the evidence that matters, and confidence. The reader should be able
to inhabit each angle without hunting the rest of the report.

Add a separate **What to do** section only when the action plan needs more room
than Overview can hold (multi-step rollout, role-specific playbooks, or long
checklists). Otherwise keep action inside Overview and skip the duplicate.

## Full briefing

Use this structure unless the user requested a custom form:

```text
## Overview

<Natural prose first: the recommendation or main answer, what to do next when
action is warranted, why it holds, and the material research limitations that
should shape how the rest is read. Write for an executive who may stop here.>

Key takeaways:
- **<Decision or next step — or, for exploratory work, what is known / open.>**
  <Supporting detail, bounds, or risk in normal text. Match intended use: action
  takeaways when the user needs next steps; knowledge/open questions when not.>
- **<Takeaway in one sentence.>** <Supporting detail.>

## Baseline facts

- <definitions / actors / current state / foundational facts and constraints,
  with footnote markers for sourced claims>

## Perspective scan

### <Lens name>

<What this lens asked, what it found, and the concrete evidence. Enough detail
for the reader to use the angle, not a one-line gloss. End with confidence.>

### <Lens name>

<...>

## Key tensions

- **<Tension in one sentence.>** <Evidence on each side, why it matters for the
  decision, and what remains unresolved.>

## Agreement and gaps

- **<Shared finding or gap in one sentence.>** <What every completed lens
  supported, or what none addressed, and why that matters.>

## Most reliable findings

- **<Finding in one sentence.>** <Foundation, mechanism, or wider effect when
  material; support from lenses/sources via footnotes; confidence High/Medium/Low.>

## What to do

<Optional. Use only when the action plan needs more room than Overview. Ordered
steps, role-specific playbooks, or long checklists. Omit when Overview already
carries the next steps.>

## What would change this most

<One question whose answer would most change the recommendation, confidence, or
next action. This is not optional trivia; it is the highest-value open question
left by the research.>

## Reliability check

- **Weakest claim:** <claim + why weak>
- **Source or bias risk:** <one-sided sources, bias carried into the briefing,
  missing counter-source>
- **Missing perspective:** <failed or underrepresented lens/source>
- **What to verify next:** <evidence that would change confidence>

## Notes

1. <source or permitted resource identifier> — <claim it supports>
2. <...>
```

## Citations

Treat sources as numbered notes. In the body, mark claims with a note number
(for example `[1]`). List the notes once at the end under **Notes**. Prefer
primary sources. Do not dump a free-floating bibliography without in-text
markers.
