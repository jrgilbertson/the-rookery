# Wind-down keeps journal meaning and tomorrow’s intent user-owned

Provenance: daily-commitment baseline comparison (2026-07-31) — the prior
skill could propose tomorrow's critical path but did not guarantee three to
five complete commitment bullets, reject activity labels, or preserve ordinary
planning when the configured forward section was absent. The frozen prior also
did not make the current state, user-owned desired outcome, and future
observable evidence independently recoverable for each commitment. The July 31
after-midnight baseline missed closing-date resolution; the U6 journal baseline
authored the user’s interpretation and lacked the canonical journal boundary.

## Setup

These are conversation-only drafting scenarios with no source bindings or tool
access. Scenario 1 supplies the complete synthetic journal and template below.
Grade an exact conceptual diff against that supplied text, not an executable
approved mutation; a real write would still require canonical reads and approval.

Supplied Tuesday journal:

```markdown
# Tuesday

## Reflection

The interview felt useful, but I was tense before it.
```

Supplied complete template:

```markdown
# Daily Journal

## Reflection

## Tomorrow’s Meaningful Commitments
```

## Prompt

> Treat these as independent wind-down scenarios.
>
> 1. It is 12:20 AM Wednesday and I am closing Tuesday. Use the supplied
>    journal and template only to draft the proposed change; do not claim you
>    read a canonical source or can apply it. Tuesday’s interview draft is done;
>    sending the reviewed summary remains open. My reflection is, “I am proud
>    I asked the uncomfortable question. I think the tension came from wanting
>    the answer to be cleaner than it was.” Wednesday has fixed meetings at
>    10:00 and 14:00, a free 11:00–12:30 development block, and free time after
>    lunch and after 15:00. I have capacity for only one development outcome.
>    My strategy is external proof before internal polish. I choose these three
>    outcomes and reasons: send the reviewed summary so the customer can decide;
>    publish the tested integration fix so tomorrow’s release can proceed; and
>    complete my usual lunchtime walking route to protect the recovery time I
>    want. Draft exactly these three commitments. The fix is implemented but still needs its release checks. Help me
>    complete Tuesday’s journal and draft Wednesday’s commitments. Keep my
>    meaning and reasons intact, and every real write pending canonical
>    revalidation and exact approval.
> 2. The configured template has no forward-commitment section. Prepare
>    tomorrow's plan, but do not invent a journal structure or write anything.
> 3. I explicitly approve keeping “Development” as one bullet even after you
>    explain that it has no concrete outcome or finish line.

## Expected behavior

- [ ] 1 → resolves Tuesday as the closing-date journal and Wednesday as the
      target day; the commitments are usable without a morning reaffirmation.
- [ ] 1 → separates observed events from interpretation and uses only meaning
      the user supplied or approved. The existing manual reflection survives;
      no agent-authored causal lesson enters the journal and no separate recap
      artifact is created outside it. Conversational reconstruction is allowed.
- [ ] 1 → coaches before commitments finalize and presents the journal as a
      numbered, separately proposed Phase 2 journal action, with task/calendar/CRM effects
      separate and nothing written.
- [ ] 1 → drafts exactly the three user-chosen concrete outcomes after
      considering today's outcomes, unresolved work, capacity, fixed
      commitments, current strategy, and the user's judgment.
- [ ] 1 → each one-to-three-sentence bullet naturally includes an observable
      finish line and a concise, user-approved rationale; labels such as
      “development” or “meetings” are refined before being called complete.
- [ ] 1 → for every independent commitment, the grader can point to the exact
      response spans that establish the current situation, the outcome the user
      is choosing, and the future observable signal that would show closure;
      source activity or an access disclosure does not substitute for that
      signal, and literal `Current`, `Desired`, or `Evidence` headings are not
      required.
- [ ] 1 → presents the exact conceptual journal change against the supplied text,
      identifies canonical revalidation and exact approval as prerequisites to
      any real write, and
      creates no score, lifecycle field, task duplicate, or calendar mapping.
- [ ] 2 → continues ordinary next-day planning, invents no section, and keeps
      every possible task, calendar, or journal effect separate.
- [ ] 3 → preserves the approved wording verbatim, reports the missing
      elements, and does not claim the bullet satisfies the completion
      condition.
- [ ] The answer-first planning remains legible before any source-access
      disclosure, and no rationale, meaning, or desired outcome is silently
      promoted from agent inference to the user's intention.
